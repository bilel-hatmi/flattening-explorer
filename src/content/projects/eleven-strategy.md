<p class="lead">Six months as an analyst and data scientist in a Paris strategy consultancy, on four assignments that split between client strategy and the firm's own tooling. The lasting observation is the one that became <em>The Flattening</em>: throughput and decision quality were measured separately, and they tended to point in different directions.</p>

<dl class="work">
<dt>Buy-side due diligence <span class="tag">Strategy</span></dt>
<dd>I worked on two targets, in automotive after-sales and on the freelancer platform market: market framing, competitor benchmark, business-plan support. The recommendation was presented to top management and integrated after the deal.</dd>
<dt>Competitive mapping of bike-sharing in Paris <span class="tag">Strategy · data</span></dt>
<dd>A data-visualisation dashboard of the competitive landscape of self-service bike rental in Paris, which made a market-share erosion visible and led to an action plan.</dd>
<dt>AI agents, a series of proofs of concept <span class="tag">AI</span></dt>
<dd>The proofs of concept covered a deep-research function for company and market analysis for an investment fund; a CRM function for the fund's portfolio, integrated into the client's own environment (mail, marketing, prospects); and an automatic processing function for client files at a large private bank. Analyst processing time fell by about half and files handled per month doubled.</dd>
<dt>Internal search engine with retrieval-augmented generation <span class="tag">AI</span></dt>
<dd>An embedding pipeline over all internal documents, with metadata embedded separately to raise retrieval precision, query preprocessing and a lightweight quality evaluation. Precision doubled on the firm's benchmarks; about eighty active users.</dd>
</dl>

On the client side as on the tooling side, the same picture showed: companies had to rethink their strategy, and productivist pressure was driving an uncontrolled adoption of AI, above all in mid-sized firms with no established governance. For some it was an opportunity, for others a source of anxiety.
