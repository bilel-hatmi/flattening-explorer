The central argument is uncomfortable: unmanaged AI adoption makes organisations better on average while making them more fragile at the extremes. Productivity gains show up on dashboards. Tail risk accumulates elsewhere, hidden by the same metrics that report the improvement.

## What the model does

A Monte Carlo model of an organisation making quarterly decisions with and without AI, under three governance regimes. It couples three mechanisms that are usually studied apart: workforce substitution, the homogenisation of judgment as people converge on the same tool, and the resilience of the institution when a shared error surfaces. In the central case, expected loss falls by 38% and output rises by 54%, while the worst quarter in a hundred gets 56% worse. The governance lever that works best is structurally simple: the person records an independent position before seeing the AI's output.

## What is here

The prize essay, an A0 poster, and an interactive explorer. The explorer lets you move through the argument rather than read about it: pick an organisational profile, follow four acts (the paradox, its mechanisms, the governance levers, and why delay makes each of them more costly), then run your own parameter combinations in the laboratory. The simulation engine runs in the browser.

The essay was one of four finalists of the Cambridge–McKinsey Risk Prize 2026, at the Cambridge Centre for Risk Studies, Judge Business School.
