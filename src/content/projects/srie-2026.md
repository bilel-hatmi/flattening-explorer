SRIE runs eight-week research placements in AI safety for Cambridge mathematics undergraduates. Since July 2026 I supervise seven of them, in groups of three, two and two, on three projects I designed around one question: what does AI do to the people who use it? The projects share a method more than a topic. Each starts from a construct that is usually discussed loosely (sycophancy, neutrality, understanding) and asks what it would take to measure it.

The week before the projects began was a general training on a single point: the conditions under which a trait measured in a language model is valid at all. A score is not a measurement until it has been shown to be stable, to separate what it claims to separate, and to survive a change of instrument. The three projects put that standard to work.

## Sycophancy as a set of constructs

Sycophancy is treated not as one behaviour but as four separately caused quantities: the direction of an answer, the conviction with which it is held, the validity of the trajectory the model follows when challenged, and the dispersion of its answers across equivalent prompts. Each is read by two instruments, observed behaviour and the model's internals (linear probes and persona vectors), across five conditions. The point is to see which quantities move together, which do not, and whether the two instruments agree. The design was reviewed by Vardhan Kumar Ray.

## Mediation under confidentiality constraints

Two parties, each with information the other must not see, and a language model between them. The mediator's neutrality is not a promise in a system prompt; it is a property of the objective function, and the project builds the game-theoretic model that makes it one. From there: an agentic workflow that implements the mediator, dials for resolution, leakage and manipulation, and a proof of concept on synthetic personas.

## An AI tutor against cognitive surrender

An adaptive tutor that measures the learner instead of merely answering. The learner is modelled as a knowledge space with a metacognitive component; the domain is constructed by LLM personas; the tutor estimates the learner's state by Bayesian updating and chooses its next question by information gain. The constraint that gives the project its name: the tutor must teach without deskilling, which rules out most of what a chatbot does by default.

## How the supervision runs

Weekly group meetings and one-to-one sessions, a shared reading protocol, sprints with written decisions, and a rule that what is not written down does not exist from one week to the next.
