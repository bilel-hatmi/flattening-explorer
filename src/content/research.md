I want to make my trajectory a bridge, to develop a vision still little expressed in AI safety.

## Vision

Today, empirical research defines the constructs or personality traits of LLMs by equivalence with human cognition. That equivalence is much debated, and understandably so: the LLM reproduces the measurement, or the surface, of a human construct, with dynamics that have no reason to resemble it. Sycophancy, for instance, has long been measured against the human sycophantic pathology. Although the analogy with human cognition can serve in many respects to understand LLMs, **modelling these constructs must rest on a causal intuition and return to mathematical properties independent of any anthropological logic.** It is the only way to obtain constructs that are ontologically and epistemologically valid for LLMs.

The regimes that emerge from these structures invite **a decentralised view of AI, steerable parametrically** to fit different contexts: education, where the preservation of metacognitive skills should become a norm; health, where the procedural validity of AI should be maximised; the general user, whom it matters to confront with the cognitive diversity of the human species. **These are not performance criteria but trade-offs**, meant to adapt the use of AI to heterogeneous spheres.

## The risks that deserve the effort

I think **the most catastrophic risks are those whose dynamics are the smoothest**. For the most spectacular risks (misuse for bio or cyber weapons, a misaligned system causing irreversible harm, the loss of oversight over a critical system), counter-reaction and public outrage will allow coordinated action and, at the level of governance, compromises hitherto unthinkable, as Covid showed: the world, for all its protocolar heaviness, revealed a surprising agility.

Economic risks, and those tied to cognitive erosion, are far more insidious: if they unfold over generations, their direction is near certain and their cost would be irreversible. The productivist logic in which AI is anchored pushes actors towards fast returns and short-term goals, favouring capital and alienating the human. The individual productivity gain is admittedly clear on average, up to a doubling: each individual becomes better. The problem is that **the concentration of models makes everyone "very good" in the same way, so that when one individual is wrong, all individuals are wrong**. Transpose this to financial actors relying on the same strategies and you recover the logic of the subprime crisis, which was in fact **a monoculture of modelling**: every bank relied on Gaussian-copula models to estimate credit defaults, which correlated the bad decisions of otherwise very heterogeneous regions of the United States.

The productivist logic is not limited to the risk of recession: it is philosophically deeper and leads to a loss of cognitive diversity in the population. What makes the human species robust today is not that humanity acts through unilateral decisions that are good on average, but that **diversity creates resilience to rare events**. That diversity would erode first through deference to AI under productivist pressure, then through the adoption of an AI-specific mode of thinking that would gradually make us lose our metacognitive skills.
